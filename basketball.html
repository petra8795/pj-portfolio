Making basketball feel like basketball.

I took over a 2.5D basketball renderer that was running basketball rules on a ported soccer engine, and turned it into a written, verifiable reference for what the product should look and move like.

01 — Why it happened

Verbal decisions evaporate

Design decisions had been agreed in meetings: player names and numbers, a possession marker, a free-throw camera. Nothing was written down. The build needed to become the written record, a visual reference for developers and a review artefact for stakeholders.

Underneath was a bigger problem: basketball rules on a soccer movement skeleton. Constant-speed running gave it away, and a geometry bug made three-pointers mathematically impossible.

02 — What I did

Spec first, then five verified chunks

Wrote the contract

01
  • A spec of target behaviour and an as-built of how it actually behaves, read at the start of every session.

Reworked movement

02
  • Speed gears, halfcourt spacing with at least one cutter moving, man-to-man defence with help rotation, transition and rebound crashes.
  • An acceptance script checks game numbers (shooting %, fouls, pace) against target bands.

Built free throws

03
  • A full multi-attempt sequence with a correct lane line-up and three camera angles that glide in and out, never cut.

Made the court ground truth

04
  • The painted court was misread three times, so every landmark became one documented constant, proven unchanged by identical before/after runs.
03 — Lessons that are now structural

How this changed the way I work

1

Verbal → written

A spec, an as-built and a dated log are the contract. They outlive the meeting.

2

Measure, don't re-derive

Geometry lives in one place with its source, not in whatever the paint suggests.

3

Verify the real thing

Check the live composed product, not a scratchpad. A stale screenshot once cost ninety minutes on correct code.
