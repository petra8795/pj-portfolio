Gas Bomber.

A phone-first arena party game where the bombs are farts. Drop beans, dodge the drifting gas, last one breathing wins. The wind is the twist: every cloud drifts, so you can get caught by your own fart if you run the wrong way.

01 — The idea

Readable chaos on a phone

Classic arena bombing, but the explosion is only half the danger. Each burst leaves a cloud the wind carries across the board; rocks and crates stop it, so you hide behind them. Rounds last about ninety seconds, with a surprise event at 0:45 and a closing gas snake at 1:20 so every round ends.

02 — Design by numbers

Tuned with headless playtests

1

No-winner rounds

First test: 41–46% of rounds ended with everyone fainted. A last-to-faint rule and tiny hidden lung differences took it to 0–5%.

2

Overpowered matches

Matches decided 60% of rounds. Nerfed from instant knockout to 60% breath, now 15–20%.

3

Bots with personality

Easy bots hesitate and panic; Hard bots kick beans and light matches the moment you're in gas. Win rates: 63%, 30%, 20%.

03 — What it taught me

Game feel is interaction design

Every rule was a design decision with a reason behind it: gas that slid around blocks felt like it chased you, so it stopped; a fan that changed the wind broke the promise that wind changes are announced, so it pushes gas instead. The same thinking feeds into my day job.
